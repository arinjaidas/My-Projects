# Methods

## Review design

The source manuscript is an analytical narrative review. It combines selected household-economic theory, behavioural explanations, published research and historical case discussions. It does not collect a new household sample, run a new regression, estimate a causal recession effect or claim exhaustive systematic coverage.

The organising distinction is between a precautionary motive and the capacity to save. The budget identity `saving = disposable income - consumption` allows greater desired precaution to coexist with lower realised saving when income falls sufficiently. Liquidity, wealth, credit, income persistence and institutions condition the response.

## Evidence and measurement

US personal saving is measured relative to disposable personal income. The Greek percentage is the share of households in an income group reporting spending below income, using separate survey samples. Indian saving components are percentages of GNDI. Indian PFCE is an aggregate constant-price consumption measure. These cannot be pooled into one common cross-country saving-rate ranking.

The reproduction script uses four archived appendix CSVs. It recomputes consumption indices relative to their documented base year and checks the reported rounded indices within 0.015 points. It then redraws a four-panel descriptive overview. Original publication vintages remain attached to the data. The manuscript retains its conceptual figure; five empirical figures are redrawn from the same appendix observations to ensure readable, unclipped labels.

The Greek Table 11 values and the RBI May 2025 saving-component table were spot-checked against their primary sources during portfolio preparation. This does not amount to a fresh verification of all 35 references or every statistical observation in the review.
