# Research design

The project brings together household-economic theory and behavioural explanations for saving, consumption and financial risk-taking during recessions. Its organising distinction is between the desire to build a precautionary buffer and the capacity to finance that buffer.

The research asks how income persistence, liquid assets, debt, credit access and policy support condition observed responses. It treats changes in portfolio holdings cautiously, since prices, beliefs, constraints and preferences can all affect those holdings.

The historical cases are selected for analytical contrast rather than as a controlled experiment. The study is a narrative review, with the limits that purposive selection entails. A future empirical extension would need longitudinal household evidence, consistent measurements and explicit identifying assumptions.

US personal saving, a Greek survey indicator of expenditure below income, and Indian saving components use different denominators and concepts. The design preserves those definitions instead of treating all three as one common cross-country saving rate.

The full manuscript, archived aggregate appendix and reproduction materials are published alongside this design note. No participant-level household data are distributed. The review does not estimate new regressions or causal effects.
